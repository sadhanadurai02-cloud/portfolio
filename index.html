Line 04 — how the call actually goes

The seven
beats

Not a framework I read. The order I've landed on after a few thousand conversations. CLICK A BEAT →

01

Research

I read before I dial. Who they are, what changed recently, who actually signs. Twenty minutes here saves the whole call.

02

Prospect

A list is not a pipeline. I build the list myself so I know why every name is on it.

03

Connect

First twelve seconds. Say something true about them, or hang up on yourself.

04

Diagnose

Questions before features. Pain before pitch. Timeline, priorities, who else is involved. I'd rather find out it's a no now than later.

05

Qualify

Not every lead is a deal. If the pieces aren't there, I'd rather know early.

06

Follow up

Most of my wins are on the fourth touch. The CRM isn't admin, it's memory.

07

Convert

By now it should feel obvious to both of us. If closing feels like pushing, I missed something at beat four.

Line 04b — a live sample

The sequence
I'd run

DAY 1 Email Subject line is their words, not mine — something from their careers page or a Navigator trigger. Three sentences: what I noticed, what it usually costs teams like theirs, one question. No deck, no calendar link yet.
DAY 2 Call Morning, before their calendar fills. I reference the email in the first line so it isn't a cold open twice. If it's a voicemail, I leave one — twelve seconds, my name, the one question, nothing else.
DAY 4 LinkedIn Connect with a note that isn't a pitch. Usually a comment on the thing I referenced on day one. This is the touch that makes the next email get opened.
DAY 6 Email + proof One customer story from a company that looks like theirs, two lines long, with the number in it. Then the ask: fifteen minutes, and I name two slots instead of asking when they're free.
DAY 9 Break-up Short, no guilt, no "just circling back". I close the loop, say I'll stop, and ask who owns this if it isn't them. That last question books more meetings than the other four touches combined.
